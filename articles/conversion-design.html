Designing for conversion: less is always more

Ahmad AlawiehApril 20265 min read

Most websites fail not because of bad code, but because of cluttered design. Visitors make a decision about your site in 50 milliseconds — before they read a single word.

One goal per page

Every page should have exactly one primary action. If a visitor can do five things, they'll do none. Identify the single outcome you want and remove everything that doesn't serve it.

Visual hierarchy is everything

The most important element should be the most visually dominant. Size, colour, whitespace, and position all signal importance. If everything is bold, nothing is.

Whitespace is a feature, not waste

Don't fill every pixel. Generous spacing makes content readable and creates a premium feel. Apple's website is 70% whitespace — that's intentional.

CTAs should say what happens next

Not "Submit" — "Start Your Project". Not "Learn More" — "See How It Works". Every button should complete the sentence "I want to…"

Audit your friction points

Every extra field, unnecessary step, or confusing label costs you conversions. Audit your forms and flows regularly — can anything be removed without losing value?

The best-designed website isn't the prettiest — it's the one where visitors effortlessly do what you want them to do.
